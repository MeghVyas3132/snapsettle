# 02 · Decisions (append-only)

## 2026-11-15
- **Idea: settlement for intents.** Chosen over a bridge circuit breaker (LedgerLock already covers it) and a LayerZero DVN (LayerZero's problem, not Reactive's) because it answers Reactive's own open question (docs/00).
- **Static `OrderData` (8 words), EVM addresses.** Cheap to decode inside `react()`; ERC-7683's resolver layer can describe it later without changing settlement.
- **The reactive contract validates fills by terms hash.** The destination cannot see the origin, so `fill` only enforces deadline, destination and once-per-(id, hash); the reactive contract compares the hash with `Opened`.
- **Fills keyed by (orderId, termsHash)** on the destination and for parked fills on Lasna: a griefer filling with fake terms cannot block the genuine fill.
- **Escrow restricted to one destination** (chain + settler, immutable). Otherwise an order the reactive contract ignores could never be refunded.
- **Callbacks are idempotent and never revert on state mismatch** (reverts are billed). `CallbackSkipped` is emitted instead.
- **`retry` is owner-only.** Every callback is billed to the escrow; an open retry would let anyone drain its callback budget.
- **Cron10 is subscribed on demand** and released when the queue empties (idle cron ticks are billed).
- **At most 3 settles per Cron tick.** Measured: 8 per tick = 1.59M gas with the mock system (over the 900k cap); 3 per tick ≈ 632k.
- **Refunds are user-initiated after `fillDeadline + refundGrace`.** No scanning loop on Lasna; `refundGrace` covers the observation lag of a last-second fill.
- **Omni mock system contract in tests**, because `reactive-test-lib` only models the legacy RVM system (`0x…fffFfF`, rvmId).

## 2026-11-15 (autonomous build)
- **Repo stays local until ~28 Nov, then goes public** as `MeghVyas3132/snapsettle` (authorised by the owner). Reason: the idea is uncontested today, and an early public repo lets ~20 track rivals see it for two weeks. The rules only require the repo to be public at submission.
- **Local three-chain harness first** (three anvil nodes with the real chain IDs + a TypeScript relay that emulates Reactive's subscribe/react/callback). Reason: the solver, bench and dashboard can be built and tested end to end before the owner funds a deployer. All local results are labelled LOCAL and never presented as live evidence.
- **Deployer wallet and faucets are the owner's job.** Agents must not create or handle keys, and faucets sit behind CAPTCHAs.
- **Testnet finality tiers: ≤ 1 USDC on sight, ≤ 10 USDC after 30 s, above after 300 s** (env-overridable in the deploy script). Reason: the bench must run 50+ loops per tier on faucet-funded Circle USDC; the shape of the policy (instant / short / long) is what the evidence needs to show, not mainnet-sized thresholds.
- **Refund grace 60 s.** It must exceed the worst fill→observation lag (seconds) with a wide margin, and it must be short enough to show "refund ignored after a valid fill" on-chain: a 300 s-tier order filled at once is still queued when the refund window opens.

## 2026-11-15 (live setup)
- **Agent-created testnet wallets** (owner approved in chat): deployer, user and solver generated with `cast wallet new`. Keys are only in gitignored files (`.secrets/deployer.env`, `offchain/.env`, mode 600) and are never printed. The owner funds the deployer alone; `pnpm fund distribute` hands gas and USDC to the user and solver and buys lREACT from the faucet.
- **Live tier thresholds lowered to ≤ 0.5 USDC on sight, ≤ 2 USDC after 30 s, above after 300 s** (bench amounts 0.25 / 1.25 / 2.5). Reason: 150+ live loops on faucet USDC. With automatic rebalancing (the solver's Base USDC goes back to the user, the user's Unichain USDC back to the solver), ~10 USDC per chain covers every tier.

## 2026-11-16 (live measurement)
- **Headline "fill → solver repaid" uses the chains' own block timestamps** (instant tier: p50 1 s, p95 2 s, 1 s resolution). The observer's wall clock (p50 4.2 s, p95 6.5 s) is shown next to it as an upper bound. Reason: the wall clock is inflated by public-RPC visibility lag; for "fill → seen on Lasna", the Lasna RPC reported events ~4 s after their block timestamps.
- **Full loop counted in Base Sepolia blocks** (measured 0.200 s/block): p50 7.2 s, p95 10.2 s. This is the most RPC-independent measure of open → repaid.
- **Dashboard derives Base/Unichain block times from block numbers** (200 ms / 1 s, measured on-chain) instead of one `getBlock` per event, which tripped the public RPC's 429 rate limit.
- **Every log scanner reads at most 190 blocks per call, and RPC errors never stop it.** Base Sepolia's public RPC rejects `eth_getLogs` over 200 blocks (HTTP 413). The first tier-2 run's observer asked for up to 500 blocks once it fell behind, so it stopped seeing events. In the same window, an RPC timeout crashed the solver bot. Both are fixed (`hub.ts`, `solver.ts`).
- **The interrupted tier-2 run is kept, not deleted.** All 24 recorded loops settled on chain. The 6 whose `Settled` the observer missed were completed from chain logs with `pnpm recover backfill` and marked `settleSeenBy: "backfill"`: exact block-time segments, no wall-clock settle time. The original observer output is kept as `loops.observed.jsonl`. A fresh 50-loop tier-2 run is the headline. Reason: dropping a run that went wrong would hide exactly the kind of thing judges should see.
- **Orders stranded by the dead solver are refunded through the protocol** (`pnpm recover refund`: `requestRefund`, then Reactive's refund callback), not left locked. This also adds live refund evidence in `evidence/live/recovery/`.

## 2026-11-16 (site redesign, live demo)
- **Dashboard rebuilt as a scroll-told 3D site** (owner chose direction A: a tournament chess clock in fog that opens to show the settlement circuit). Three.js, postprocessing (+ N8AO ambient occlusion at full resolution: its half-res path breaks on three r187), GSAP ScrollTrigger + SplitText, Lenis. Every number on it still comes from evidence JSON or live chain reads.
- **Graphite clock, not white**: a white object on white fog had no tonal range and read as generic. Real tournament clocks are dark; a graphite body with a brushed faceplate and backlit LCDs gives contrast and a material story.
- **UI components ported, not imported**: Magic UI / React Bits patterns (Marquee, Number Ticker, Animated List, Border Beam, Text Animate, Magnet) are React + Tailwind. The page is vanilla TypeScript, so each is a small native module in `dashboard/src/ui.ts`. Generic effects (gradient beams, sparkles, glass cards) were left out.
- **Live, not only recorded** (owner request): "Open your own order" (browser wallet, 0.25 USDC on Base Sepolia, the judge receives the output on Unichain) and "Run one live order" via `offchain/src/demo.ts` (heartbeat every 15 min, on-demand orders rate-limited: one per 40 s, 4 per hour per visitor, 150 per day). Anything live is labelled "Live on testnet"; the recorded loop is labelled "Replay". The idle right LCD shows the recorded median as "P50", never "LIVE".
- **Demo budget**: an instant-tier order costs about three reactive transactions; the tier-2 run (cron ticks while waiting) used ~5.9 lREACT for 50 loops. A 15-minute heartbeat plus capped on-demand orders fits the deployer's lREACT plus the Sepolia faucet through judging.
- **Per-tier numbers are pooled across that tier's live runs** (same contracts, same policy): instant 102/102, 30 s 101/101, 5 min 109/112. Each run stays in `evidence/live/` with its own summary. Reason: every single run is honest but small; pooling gives the 50+ per tier the brief asks for without hiding any run.
- **The third 5-minute run used concurrency 8, too high for the test USDC in circulation**: 13 opens reverted before an order existed, and 3 orders our solver could not fill (its Unichain inventory ran out) were refunded by Reactive's callback via `pnpm recover refund`. Lesson recorded: at a 5-minute settle delay, in-flight capital is concurrency × amount on both chains.
- **Faster solver** (parallel fills, fixed gas, background fee refresh, one log query per poll): open → fill p50 1.2 s / p95 2.0 s, whole loop p50 3.2 s / p95 4.0 s over 52/52 live loops (was 7.2–8.4 s / 10.2–15.6 s). The site shows solver-dependent legs from the newest run and Reactive's legs pooled across all runs, so the solver upgrade is neither hidden nor mixed into older numbers.

## 2026-11-16 (v2, external review)
- **v2 contracts**: delayed tiers settle from `DestinationSettler.Confirmed` (sent by the solver after the delay; re-reads the fill in Unichain's state), not from a Cron10 timer. The external review was right that "no cron" and "a re-check after the delay" cannot both hold without some actor: we chose the solver's `confirm` transaction and say so. On-sight orders still need no actor after the fill.
- **Outflow cap** in `OriginEscrow` (500 USDC per hour on testnet): bounds what a faulty Reactive could release; over-cap settlements are deferred, never lost.
- **Numbers re-based**: Across's challenge period is read on-chain (HubPool `liveness()` = 1800 s); the 2 h figure came from stale docs. Fill → repaid is reported from four clocks with their biases (JUDGE.md §3); the "1 s" headline was below its own resolution and is gone. The capital comparison is per tier (about 6–24× for the 300 s tier), never one big multiple.
- **Only current-contract runs feed headline numbers**; v1 runs stay in `evidence/live/`, labelled.
- **Solver scan bug (introduced by the speed-up, found during the v2 bench)**: when the cursor fell >200 blocks behind, every getLogs was refused (HTTP 413), and the recovery only read the first line of the error, so it never fired. Three tier-0 orders were never filled; the bench timed out on them after 10 minutes and they were refunded through Reactive. Fixed: the head is tracked in a background loop and every query is capped at 190 blocks. Recorded in the run as it happened.
